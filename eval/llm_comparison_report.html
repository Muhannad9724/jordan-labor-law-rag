What the judge caught that citation-matching missed

Checking whether the right article number was cited says nothing about whether the answer stayed on-topic or avoided embellishment. Two real defects only showed up once someone actually read the answers.

Relevancy failure

"The question asked about Germany's minimum wage, which the answer cannot provide, so it substitutes unrelated Jordanian information instead."

Cohere, out-of-domain trap question — scored relevancy 2/5. It correctly avoided inventing a German figure, but padded the refusal with unrequested Jordan minimum-wage detail instead of just declining.

Faithfulness failure

"The core legal claim is accurate, but the response includes a stray Chinese sentence that is an unexplained artifact not derived from the sources."

Qwen 2.5-72B, annual-leave-splitting question — scored faithfulness 3/5. A generation-quality bug with nothing to do with citations; the cheap open-weight route's real risk.

Also worth noting, against my own methodology: the regex citation-check flagged Qwen as "wrong" on the 1999-law question (it extracted "Article 11," not the ground-truth "10"). The judge read the actual answer — Qwen had written "Article 10/11" together and was factually accurate — and scored it 5/5 faithfulness. The shallow proxy produced a false negative here; the judge caught it.
